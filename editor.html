Ooha ArtsThe Editor

The edit is the whole work.

Every photograph that leaves this studio has been looked at four hundred times. This is the person doing that, and the reason it matters.

01 — Introduction

Fifteen yearsbehind the glass.

Ooha Arts — Founder & Principal Editor. I started as a wedding photographer who was tired of beautiful days turning into thin, over-bright folders.

So I learned the other half of the job. Composition is cheap. Timing is harder. Deciding which four frames out of nine thousand deserve to exist — that is the craft, and almost nobody teaches it.

Ooha Arts exists because I wanted one studio where the person who takes the photograph is also the person who chooses the final one. Nothing gets lost in a handover, nothing gets "fixed" by someone who never saw the brief, and nothing leaves that I would not put on my own wall.

02 — What I believe

Three rules, held for eleven years.

01

Subtract until it hurts

If a frame needs three things to work, it needs one. We delete first and rescue second, never the other way around.

02

One light, one look

Every series we deliver shares a single grade. Consistency across forty images matters more than any one of them being perfect.

03

Tell the truth, warmly

Retouching should remove distractions, never evidence of a person. Pores, laugh lines and dust stay — they are the photograph.

04 — Anatomy of an edit

How 4,000 framesbecome 40.

The cull

Contact sheet first, no corrections. Anything that is a repeat, a stumble or a soft frame leaves the pool before I look at a single histogram.

The correction

Lens and geometry first, exposure second, white balance third. Always in that order, always non-destructively.

The grade

One curve for the whole series, then per-image work that stays inside it. Split-toning, density, and a grain layer to bind the frames together.

The retouch

Skin cleaned, not smoothed. Dust, distracting signage and the odd photobomber removed. Nothing that changes what the frame is saying.

The delivery

Masters as 16-bit TIFF, a sRGB set for the web, square and vertical crops for social, and a print file with a soft proof attached.
